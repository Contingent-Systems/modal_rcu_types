> All reviewers: Mechanization, and corrections to the published invariants


Since submission we have mechanized the global invariants, the reachability core
of the soundness argument, and every atomic action in full, in Rocq (about
16,300 lines and 905 results, axiom-free).  The build refuses to succeed if
the development contains an admitted proof, or if any of its hundred and
ninety assumption checks reports an axiom rather than closed under the
global context;
we mention this because a Rocq file that compiles proves considerably less than
it looks.  This turned up more than we expected, and we think it materially
strengthens the paper.

Ten of the WellFormed invariants as published are defective:

- Unique-Reachable is vacuous as written, and admits heaps that are not trees.
- Readers-Iterators-Only is contradictory in any state containing both a reader
  and a freshly allocated node, so it can be used to derive anything.
- Fresh-Reachable is satisfied by its own hypothesis, and so constrains nothing.
- Fresh-Points-Iterator contradicts Fresh-Not-Reader.
- Unlinked-Reachability does not chain: its conclusion is weaker than its
  hypothesis, so the induction along a path stalls at the first freeable
  predecessor.
- Heap-Domain-Closure is false as stated.  It constrains every edge in the
  heap, but T-UnlinkH leaves the unlinked node still pointing at its old child
  and nothing orders the reclamation of two unlinked nodes, so a well-typed
  section may free a node that another, itself awaiting reclamation, still
  points at.  The pointer dangles harmlessly, in a node that is detached and
  unreachable -- which is exactly the exemption No-Sharing already carries and
  this one does not.
- Fresh-Not-Reader is too weak.  It denies a freshly allocated node the
  *iterator* and *unlinked* observations and says nothing about *freeable*, and
  that third case is the one T-Replace and T-Insert need.  Both link a fresh
  node under a reference the writer holds as an iterator; the write creates that
  edge, and Unlinked-Reachability then requires that if the new child is
  unlinked or freeable the parent is too -- which Writer-Unlink denies of an
  iterator.  So the obligation can be discharged only by denying both to the
  child, and the published invariant denies one.

  This one differs in kind from the others, and we state the difference rather
  than paper over it: the reachable states contain no such node, since a node
  becomes freeable only from unlinked, and the unlinked conjunct already denies
  that.  The property holds inductively over whole executions.  What fails is
  locality -- the action lemma is not provable from the invariants alone, which
  is the only thing a global invariant set is for.  Adding the conjunct restores
  it and costs nothing: SyncStop, the one action that could have been asked to
  pay for it, discharges the new conjunct from the old one.  We give a state
  satisfying the other eighteen invariants and the published form in which a
  node is both fresh and freeable, and a proof that linking it breaks
  Unlinked-Reachability.
- Unlinking is not said to be the writer's.  WriterFresh says an allocation
  belongs to the lock holder; nothing said the same of an unlinking, and the
  Unlinked-Reachability case of T-UnlinkH and T-Replace needs it.  That case
  ends at a detached predecessor of the node being unlinked -- No-Sharing
  supplies the detachment, the repaired premise of those rules excludes a
  rcuFresh one -- so the predecessor is unlinked or freeable by *some* thread,
  and Unlinked-Reachability's conclusion names the thread its hypothesis names.
  Like the previous item this is a failure of locality rather than a false
  statement, and it was found the same way, by attempting the case.  We give a
  state satisfying the other eighteen in which a node is unlinked by a thread
  that is not the lock holder.
- Iterator observations are not said to belong to an active thread, and this
  one is not merely a failure of locality.  SyncStart puts every unlinked node
  on the free list with the current readers as its bounding set, and
  the iterators-in-free-list invariant then demands that every thread observing
  such a node as an iterator is in that set: it is the obligation that a grace
  period waits for everyone who could still be looking.  Writer-Unlink rules out
  the writer.
  Nothing ruled out a thread that is neither the writer nor a current reader,
  and against such a thread the grace period would complete while a live
  reference remained -- which is the accident the whole structure exists to
  prevent.  The three together are a family: each kind of observation
  constrains who may hold it, WriterFresh does it for fresh, and the published
  set had exactly one of the three.  We give a state satisfying the other
  eighteen in which a node is observed as an iterator by a thread that is
  neither the writer nor a reader.
- Nothing says where free-list entries may live.  Three invariants mention the
  free list -- iterators-in-free-list, free-list-reachability and
  readers-in-free-list -- and all three are conditional on an entry existing;
  none says which nodes may have one.  Nothing in the published set therefore
  rules out an entry at a node that is not detached, not reachable, and not
  even in the heap.  SyncStart's specification says the grace period covers
  *exactly* the detached nodes, and re-establishing "exactly" needs to know
  that the entries already there were at detached nodes too; it is the one
  hypothesis of the snapshot step that no invariant supplies.  We give a state
  satisfying all nineteen with a free-list entry at a location carrying no
  observations at all.  The repair is a conjunct saying an entry implies the
  node is unlinked or freeable, which SyncStart establishes and which every
  other action either preserves trivially or -- Free and ReadEnd -- preserves
  because it only shrinks the free list.

Five of these are the consequential ones.  Unlinked-Reachability is discussed
under Delayed Ownership Transfer below; Heap-Domain-Closure is the invariant the
Free case rests on, and under the published statement that case cannot be proved
at all, which is presumably why our proof records it as trivial; and
Fresh-Not-Reader is what the two rules that link a fresh node need, so under the
published statement neither T-Replace nor T-Insert can be shown to preserve
Unlinked-Reachability; the missing statement about unlinking is what the two
rules that *unlink* a node need, for the same invariant; and the missing
statement about iterators is what SyncStart needs, without which a grace period
can complete too early.

Two more invariants are missing, and both were found by attempting the last of
the linking rules against the Iris invariant described below.  T-LinkF-Null
publishes a fresh node, and to do so it must know that no thread other than the
writer observes that node.  Two invariants ought to supply that between them and
neither does.

- Nothing says the root observation marks the root.  It is the one observation
  that carries no thread, and the published set constrains where every *tagged*
  observation may sit while saying nothing about this one, so a well-formed
  state may record it at a location that is not the root.  That also weakens
  Unique-Reachable-Tree's second part, whose conclusion is "iterator or root"
  and which is read as "every reachable node is the writer's, except the root
  itself" -- under the published set the exception is not tied to the root.
- WriterFresh is too weak.  It is stated with a stack reference in its
  hypothesis -- a thread that *names* a fresh node holds the lock -- so a fresh
  observation whose variable has left scope constrains nobody.  This corrects
  something we said above: the family of three is right, but the member the
  published set already had was the weak one, and it needs the same repair as
  the two we add.

One witness does for both: a state satisfying all nineteen in which the root
observation sits at a non-root location and a fresh observation belongs to a
thread that does not hold the lock.  Only the second repair is needed by any
proof we give, so only it is carried; the first we report, because a reader of
Unique-Reachable-Tree will assume it.

A defect in the type rules rather than in an invariant. Neither
T-Replace nor T-UnlinkH prevents a rcuFresh reference in the environment from
holding a field that points at the node the rule unlinks; the aliasing premises
quantify only over rcuItr references, and the denotation of a type environment
is an intersection rather than a separating conjunction, so nothing else
excludes it. The resulting state breaks Fresh-Points-Iterator and the denotation
of the fresh reference's own type, and linking that reference in afterwards
would splice a node already scheduled for reclamation back into the structure.
Both rules now carry a premise excluding it. Both worked examples still type
check unchanged, since the fresh node each builds points at the children of the
node being replaced rather than at that node.

Ten of the fourteen were found by attempting the proofs rather than by reading,
and seven of the ten are cases whose proof the technical report calls trivial.  We think
that is the most useful thing the exercise produced: the defects that mattered
were not the ones that looked doubtful. Every defect has
a machine-checked proof or counterexample, the corrected set is machine-checked
satisfiable, and the development has no axioms. The corrected statements now
appear as an appendix, with the published ones retained alongside for
comparison.

The development also now takes every atomic action end to end, rather than one
invariant at a time: the six RCU primitives -- ReadBegin, ReadEnd, SyncStart,
SyncStop, WriteBegin, WriteEnd -- and every type rule that changes the state:
T-Root, T-ReadS, T-ReadH, T-Alloc, T-WriteFH, T-UnlinkH, T-Replace, T-Insert,
T-LinkF-Null and T-Free.  Fifteen theorems, the three variable-binding rules
sharing one and a reader's read composing two.  The structural and subtyping
rules change no state and so carry no action lemma.  Doing the actions whole was
worth more than the sum of the cases.

We should be exact about what these theorems are, since their shape invites
overreading.  Each says an action preserves WellFormed: the global invariants
survive every atomic step.  Axiom soundness asks for more -- the post-state must
satisfy the denotation of the post-type environment, not just the invariants,
so that after T-Replace the fresh reference really is an rcuItr at the replaced
node's path and the replaced one really is unlinked.  That half is not done.
What the invariants give is the half of each case that is about the shared
structure rather than the types, which is the half the technical report treats
as trivial and the half where we found the defects.

For Free, seventeen of the eighteen follow from a single observation, that
freeing removes edges and therefore removes paths, so every invariant with a
reachability or edge hypothesis gets weaker.  Heap-Domain-Closure is the only
one whose conclusion is about the heap and the only one with any content.  That
is a fair account of what "trivial" was doing: right about seventeen eighteenths
of the case and wrong about the part that mattered.

The three heap mutations are the hardest, and for a reason that appears only
when the action is attempted in full: each writes a field *and* changes the
writer's observations, so the ten heap-free invariants, which were identities
for the other three actions, all acquire content.  The revocation an unlink
performs is of *the writer's* iterator observation and of nothing else -- the
readers still traversing the node keep theirs, which is the whole point of the
structure -- so an update replacing a location's observation set outright would
be wrong, and wrong in a way Fresh-Points-Iterator cannot see and
Reader-Writer-Observation can.  Two earlier repairs are spent here: the premise
excluding a rcuFresh predecessor of the unlinked node, twice in each unlinking
rule, and Fresh-Not-Reader's new conjunct again in the Writer-Unlink case of the
two rules that link a fresh node.

ReadEnd changed its own statement twice, and neither change is visible from any
single invariant.  It needs the departing reader's variables to leave scope in
the same step, or Reader-Writer-Observation fails immediately -- the block
structure of the read critical section supplies this, but only once it is
carried into the action's statement.  And it must not be described as "the
departing thread's entries are gone", which is the natural description and is
correct for every invariant about a thread's observations: the root observation
is anonymous, the observation map is keyed by observer, so that description
deletes the root observation and the second half of Unique-Root fails.  We give
a well-formed state where it does.  This is an encoding mismatch rather than a
defect in anything published, and we report it as such.

Readers-Iterators-Only turns out to be load-bearing in exactly one place --
ReadEnd, where it is what says the departing thread held no detaching
observation, so that dropping its entries is safe.  It is needed nowhere else
except to preserve itself.  That it is contradictory as published, in exactly
the states where it is needed, is therefore not a cosmetic defect.

One further thing about ReadEnd and WriteEnd, which we report against ourselves
because it is the failure mode a proof assistant does not catch.  Both rules
require *every* variable of the departing thread to leave scope.  Our bridge
from the abstract logical state to the mechanized one represented scope as a
finite set of variable/thread pairs that are out of scope, and with variables
drawn from an infinite supply no finite set contains every variable of a
thread.  The hypothesis was unsatisfiable, so the two theorems that carried it
-- proved, checked, axiom-free -- established nothing.  Flipping the polarity
does not help, since the initial state has nothing out of scope.  Scope is now
a predicate rather than a set, which costs nothing, and both theorems mean what
they say.  We mention it because a vacuous theorem type-checks, closes under
the global context and reports no axioms; only using it finds the problem, and
what found this one was restating the two actions against the Iris invariant
described next.

We also checked the framing property the Views Framework requires, and it does
not hold as published.  Soundness there is stated modulo composition with an
arbitrary frame, so the invariants must be closed under the composition of
logical states; they are not.  We give two well-formed states whose composition
violates No-Sharing, and the reason is structural rather than incidental: every
state carries the whole heap, so composition must reconcile two copies of it,
and an invariant that constrains two pieces of split per-thread state at once
cannot survive that.  Sharing the heap and adding locality conditions repairs
it, and we give the repaired operation together with a proof that the
invariants are closed under it and a check that the repair is not vacuous.  We
also record what is *not* a defect, since we first thought it was: interference
is declared as a subset of the state space, so closure under it is a typing
obligation rather than a proof obligation.  Composition is a function, and that
is where the real obligation lives.

The repair scales badly to more than one writer -- every write must be absorbed
by every frame, and the rely degenerates -- so we also carried out the
alternative, which is to put the heap in an Iris invariant and leave only the
observation map and free list in the separating part.  All fifteen atomic
actions are now restated in that setting, and the outcome is the useful part:
the pure action lemmas plug in unchanged.  Each restated action is its action
lemma applied once, plus a ghost update.  Ten of those updates are at a single
key; the other five are map-wide, and what they need turned out to be neither a
new camera nor a new frame-preserving update but the observation that a
map-wide change is an iterated single-key one, legal exactly when the thread
owns every fragment it touches.  Two small induction lemmas supply it.  The one
We then chose the physical-state predicate, which is what turns these steps
into Hoare triples, and that is where the state model gave way.  The standard
choice is a heap of points-to assertions, so that a thread's right to write a
field is a resource.  It was not available: allocation as published gives the
new node *every* field, and field names are drawn from an infinite supply, so
one allocation puts infinitely many cells in the heap.  No finite map
represents such a heap, so there is no camera of the usual shape over it -- they
are all built on finite maps -- and without points-to, a rule whose premise
mentions the heap cannot be stated outside the invariant at all, because the
heap is bound inside it.  We give the proof, stated about the published
definition.

This is a twelfth defect, and the repair is a class declaration: allocation
takes the node's field list and initialises exactly those fields.  The heap is
then finite if it starts finite, which we prove for all three heap-changing
operations.  The cost is local -- allocation gains an argument, heap-domain
closure is unchanged, and the allocation case gains one hypothesis, that the
field list holds every RCU field, which is what makes it a declaration rather
than an arbitrary subset and what the fresh node's denotation needs.  We have
made this change.

With a finite heap, eight actions close completely: Free, all five heap
mutations, T-Alloc, and the binding rules -- which is to say the entire write
side.  Each is stated with no parameters and nothing assumed about shared state:
the invariant is opened, every premise is *derived*, the step is taken, the
invariant is closed.

There are four kinds of premise and each is discharged differently.
Observational ones come from the thread's own ghost fragment -- exclusive
control of its entry at a node, and agreement turns that into "the node is
observed freeable", which is the side condition becoming a resource.  Physical
ones come from points-to, the lock token and the stack fragment, none of which
existed before the field list made the heap finite.  Path premises come from
owning the cells along the path; what that derivation does *not* use is the
point -- no invariant, no uniqueness, no shape condition -- because reachability
by a *named* path is a local fact about the cells on it.

Negative premises, that something is absent, cannot come from a fragment at all,
since a fragment says what is and never what is not.  They come from invariants.
"No free-list entry here" is the free-list conjunct we add, with Writer-Unlink
or, for a fresh node, Fresh-Not-Reader.  "This fresh node is unreachable" is
Fresh-Reachable with the pinned root.  "No other thread observes this node as
fresh" is the repaired WriterFresh.  "Every detaching observation is the
writer's" and "this node is not detached" are Writer-Unlink with
Fresh-Not-Reader.  We think that is a concrete answer to what the global
invariants are *for* in a separation-logic reading of this system: they are
exactly the facts a resource cannot carry -- and three of the five just named
are invariants this work adds.

Two premises are of no such kind.  The aliasing premise the unlinking rules
carry -- no rcuFresh reference anywhere points at the node being unlinked -- is
negative and is not an invariant.  What discharges it in the type system is the
environment itself, whose denotation is an intersection over all of it.  Here
the invariant records that the fresh nodes are *enumerated*, the thread holds
the enumeration and those nodes' cells, and the premise becomes a statement
about values the thread owns -- exactly what our checker's noFreshPointsAt
decides by walking the environment.  The quantifier does not disappear; it moves
from the shared state, where nothing could discharge it, to the thread's own
resources, where the type system already had it.

T-Alloc's six premises say the new location is unallocated, unreferenced,
pointed at by nothing, unobserved, unlisted and not the root.  Those look as
though they need an operational semantics -- something must *produce* a fresh
address -- and we expected to have to stop there.  They do not.  An allocator is
a function of the state, and the state is now finite in every component, so the
locations used for anything form a finite set and a location outside it
satisfies all six at once.  The field-list repair pays twice: it made the heap
representable, and it made the allocator constructible.

We have also now made a start on the other half, the type environment as a
resource, and it produced one thing worth reporting.  The obvious reading, one
resource per variable, does not work: two iterators may name the same node and
two paths overlap in their prefix, so a per-variable reading would demand two
exclusive fragments for one location and two owners for one cell.  The aliasing
side conditions are exactly what rule that out, but they are conditions on the
*environment* rather than on the individual variable, so a per-variable reading
cannot see them.

What works is to own the resources *once*, as maps -- the thread's stack
entries, its observations, its free-list entries, and a set of cells -- and make
each variable's requirement a pure condition on those maps.  A path is then a
fold of lookups in the cell map, an alias is two variables reading the same
entry, and nothing is duplicated because a map has one entry per key.  We prove
that what the writer holds implies the denotation of its type environment, for
every type the write side produces, and that this reading survives an
allocation.  A consequence worth recording: no fractional permissions are needed
on the write side, because overlapping paths share cells by being the same entry
rather than by splitting ownership.  The read side still needs them, for the
different reason that a reader and a writer must hold the same cell at once.

We then stated one rule that way end to end: T-Alloc with a type environment on
both sides, so the thread hands in the resources its environment reads as Gamma
and gets back the resources its environment reads as Gamma with the allocated
variable rebound to rcuFresh.  Nothing new is proved in it -- the step is the
closed triple's and the environment's survival is the monotonicity lemma -- but
it is the shape all fifteen should have, and the first of them to have it.

The other seven rules do not grow the maps at a new location; they overwrite an
entry, so what they need is framing rather than monotonicity: the environment
survives a step whose footprint it does not read.  There are only three kinds of
footprint -- a cell, a location's observations, a stack slot -- so there are
three lemmas rather than seven.  The cell case is where the aliasing side
conditions land: a path is a fold of lookups, so the cells it reads are a
concrete list, and a write outside that list cannot change the fold.  Its
hypothesis is exactly the question our MayAlias procedure decides.  The decision
procedure and the framing lemma are the same condition, one decided and one
proved, which is the tightest connection between the two halves of this work
that we have.

What is left for each rule is the variable it *touches*, and that needed one
thing the reading did not yet have: field maps.  Restricting types to empty
field maps made three of the post-types inexpressible -- T-WriteFH produces a
fresh node with a field written, and T-Insert and T-Replace take field maps as
premises -- so the reading now carries them, read on the thread's own maps in
the same way as everything else.  That also completes the framing lemmas'
hypotheses: a step must not touch a tracked field either.

With that, the touched variable is not a lemma but the definition supplied.  The
three cases that occur -- demotion to unlinked, to freeable, and promotion of a
fresh node to an iterator one field beyond its parent -- are in the development
so the claim is checkable rather than asserted.  Only the promotion has any
content, and the content is that the new path is the old one extended.

Making the rules compose with that reading removed the last two ad-hoc
resources: the fresh nodes had been a separate assertion, and the paths had been
one assertion per iterator.  Both are now conditions on the single cell map the
writer owns.  The path case is the instructive one -- one assertion per iterator
cannot be right, because two iterators' paths overlap in their prefix and a
points-to is exclusive.  All five mutation rules are now stated with their paths
and their fresh nodes as pure facts about that map, which is the same form the
environment reading uses, so the two sides of a rule are written in the same
language.

With that, a mutation goes end to end.  T-LinkF-Null is now stated with a type
environment on both sides: the variables the step does not touch are framed
across it by the two framing lemmas, and the one it does touch gets its new
type.  The only work in the touched case is that the fresh node's new path is
the parent's extended by the field just written, and that its chain of iterator
observations is the parent's chain plus one step.  None of that is about RCU --
it is the bookkeeping that connects a rule's resources to a rule's types, it is
the same bookkeeping for all five mutations, and having done it once the
remaining four are a transcription.

T-UnlinkH follows, and it is the transcription the first one was supposed to
make possible.  It touches three variables rather than one -- the parent's field
map is retargeted, the victim is demoted to unlinked, and the node below is
promoted to the parent's field -- and each is one of three small toolkit
lemmas, with the rest of the environment framed by the same two lemmas in the
same order.  It took no new ideas, which is what the toolkit was for.

T-WriteFH, T-Insert and T-Replace follow without incident, and with them every
heap mutation and the allocation are stated with a type environment on both
sides.  The pattern is uniform: the untouched variables are framed by the two
lemmas, and the touched ones are supplied, each being one of three forms.
T-WriteFH is the smallest, changing no observation at all; T-Insert touches
three variables, the last of which moves one step further down the structure;
T-Replace is a promotion and a demotion at once.  That the last three needed no
new machinery is the substance of the claim: what varies between the rules is
which of the three touched-variable forms occur and how many times, and what
does not vary is anything about RCU.

Two smaller things this turned up.  The denotation of freeable was stated as an
equality of *functions*, and in the ghost encoding the free-list entry is a
membership test on a finite set; proving those equal needs functional
extensionality, which we do not assume.  A denotation that cannot be established
without an axiom is a defect in the denotation, and it is stated extensionally
now.  And undef has no resource reading at all: its second conjunct is about the
free list at a node the thread no longer references, which is state it does not
own -- that conjunct belongs in an invariant rather than in a type, and we
report it rather than move it.

What is left is the read side and the protocol, and the two obstacles are
particular rather than general.  The reader's read cannot be stated at all,
because the points-to assertion is exclusive: a reader must read cells the
writer also holds.  The repair is a fractional heap, which is standard and which
changes the camera under everything above; it is worth saying plainly that the
write side needed no fractions and the read side cannot be done without them,
which is after all what read-copy-update is about.  And WriteBegin asks that the
lock is free while SyncStop asks that no thread is still bounding -- the two
points where the protocol *tests* or *waits on* shared state rather than acting
on state it owns.  Those are what an operational semantics is for, a
compare-and-set and a wait loop, and they are the only two places in the system
that need one.  The remaining four control actions need reader-set and
bounding-set tokens, which is the lock's construction done twice more.

One last thing the field-list repair exposed, which we record rather than fix.
The mutation rules are stated with "every field is an RCU field", which with a
finite field list contradicts the class declaration the allocation rule needs,
so the two cannot appear in one lemma.  The honest repair is to restrict
No-Sharing's field conditions to declared fields.

One last thing worth saying about the infinite-heap defect, because it cuts the
other way.  The checker never had it.  Fields there are a bitset over a declared
field list, which is the class declaration the repair introduces, and it is what
makes the coverage side condition decidable at all.  The model that had to be
approximated to be executable turned out to be the correct one, and the model
written for the proof was the one that was wrong.  We have added checks to the
prototype recording the correspondence.

> All reviewers: Concerns about complexity, decidability, automation


Our focus in this paper, as Reviewer 1 noted, is the core reasoning principles where we chose a type system to focus on articulating those principles. As well-stated by Reviewer 2: this type system should be general enough to capture interesting programs, and likely requires less knowledge of advanced logical concepts to apply than e.g., an Iris-based logic.


On decidability, Reviewer 2 is right about the system as presented, and we keep
that concession: a declarative derivation may insert T-Conseq anywhere, so the
declarative system is not decidable as stated.  But the question we should have
answered is the one behind it -- can the side conditions be discharged
mechanically -- and there we can now say considerably more than "future work".

Every framing side condition in the type rules is a negated MayAlias query, so
that is where decidability is decided.  On the path language as written the
general problem is one of word equations, because abstracted iteration lets the
same index occur in two places.  We give instead an *algorithmic* presentation:
a fragment of the path language (paths in which no index occurs twice, drawn
from a fixed list in increasing order), a canonical join at control-flow merges,
reindexing-by-matching at back edges, and a decision procedure for MayAlias that
is linear after a prefix reduction and then a product construction.  Subsumption
is applied only at fixed points.  Checking a critical section becomes a
terminating forward dataflow over environments in that fragment, and the
fragment is the class of programs the algorithm is complete for.

We can also characterise its precision exactly, which we think answers the part
of the question that matters for a diagnostic.  MayAlias is sound in the
direction the type system needs -- every use is under a negation, so
over-approximating rejects safe programs but never accepts unsafe ones -- and it
is *exact* whenever no path index survives the prefix reduction in both
arguments.  A syntactic condition guarantees that: all paths mentioning an index
agree on the segments before it, which is what a traversal produces anyway, since
the cursors of a loop are reindexed by the same back edge.  Measured against
brute-force enumeration over 100,000 random pairs from the fragment across five
seeds, no answer was unsound, and the procedure is exact in all 93,564 of the
pairs where no index survives the prefix reduction in both arguments.  The 6,436
that do share one carry the whole of the residual imprecision, and it is
substantial there -- the procedure agrees on 64% of them -- which is the point:
the imprecision is not spread thinly over the domain but concentrated outside
the anchored class.  A second test, over a further 100,000 pairs, is exact on
all 90,256 that are anchored.  The suites are seeded, so these are reproducible
figures rather than a sample.

Reviewer 3's intuition about automation is borne out, and further than we
expected when we drafted this.  There is a checker.

Four layers in dependency-free C++17 -- the path domain, the types and
environments, the type rules as transfer functions, and a statement IR with a
forward dataflow over it -- passing 93 unit and property checks, the latter
comparing each abstract operation against the concrete semantics by
enumeration.  On top of them a Clang front end translates a C function into the
IR through the CFG, taking back edges from the dominator tree and refinements
from the branch conditions, and reports what the dataflow finds against the
source.

It needs no annotation language, and rather less than we first claimed.  __rcu
on a field is enough to recognise the reads and the writes: they are taken from
the field's type, not from an accessor macro, so rcu_dereference and
rcu_assign_pointer need not appear at all.  rcu_read_lock/rcu_read_unlock,
synchronize_rcu and the kfree family distinguish every remaining action the type
system has a rule for; only the root has to be marked, because nothing in the
source distinguishes it.  We think that is the
more useful claim than any figure about annotation burden: the annotations are
already in the code, and what has been missing is a check of what they mean.

The binary search tree delete of the appendix, written as C, type checks --
including the two-child case that copies the successor, splices the copy in and
reclaims the original after a grace period.  We report that together with what
the checker rejects, since a file that passes and cannot be made to fail is not
evidence: the same function with one field left out of the copy is rejected by
the mirroring premise, and with the grace period removed is rejected at the
free.

What it does not do: a function is checked on its own, with its pointer
parameters assumed to be iterators, and summaries carry kinds across a call but
not paths.  The limit shows up in the same example -- the delete checks when it
reads its cursor out of the root and does not when the two cursors arrive as
parameters, because nothing at the boundary says the second is the first's
child.

As Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 pointed out, there are semantically misleading typos (ex: wrong brackets [|, missing y =x.f;C) in Figure 2 in desugaring, we will fix them. 


The Views Framework allows convenient use of only single-step *atomic* primitives.  Decomposing Sync into SyncStart and SyncStop is therefore required for our proof because the synchronization involves two steps: book-keeping, then waiting. Client programs would always execute both, together, without intervening local actions.




> R2&R3: Interactions between critical sections and non-critical-section code


The type system ensures unlinked and freeable references are handled *linearly*, as they cannot be dropped (coerced to undef). The top-level rule ToRCUWrite does ensure unlinked references have been freed, by forbidding them in the critical section's post-type-environment, as Reviewer 3 noticed. Preventing the reuse of rcuItr references across critical sections is subtler: the non-critical section system is *not* flow-sensitive, and does not include rcuItr.  So the initial environment lacks rcuItr, and "trailing" rcuItr may not escape.

> R2: Delayed Ownership Transfer


We agree this is the most delicate part of the proof, and we can now say
considerably more than we could at submission.

The mechanism is as you describe: once an object is observed as *unlinked* by
the writer in the observation map of the logical state, it waits for all other
threads' *iterator* observations to be dropped (the object becomes *freeable*
for the writer) before being reclaimed.

What makes that safe is the property that a node awaiting reclamation is never
reachable from the root, and this is exactly where the published
Unlinked-Reachability fails. It is stated one step, with hypothesis "unlinked"
and conclusion "unlinked or freeable", so an induction along a path stalls at
the first freeable predecessor, where no invariant applies. The Unlink proof
appeals to it in the reachability form regardless. Closing the hypothesis under
the disjunction repairs it; we have machine-checked both the repair and a
counterexample to the published form, along with the corresponding chaining
property for the free list. Together those two are what keep a newly arrived
reader away from a node scheduled for reclamation.

We have also mechanized the Free case of heap-domain closure, and now the whole
of Free: all eighteen invariants, for the action as a step. Free is the only
action that removes a location, so it is the only place where the obligation --
that no surviving edge points at the freed node -- is real; we give a
counterexample showing it cannot be dispensed with, and the corrected
Heap-Domain-Closure is what discharges it, from the repaired
Unlinked-Reachability. The technical report discharges that case, and the whole
group of memory axioms for that action, as "trivial".

Two further pieces of that argument are now proved rather than asserted.  The
claim that a node awaiting reclamation is not reachable is stated directly, once
for each rule that produces one -- unlinking and replacement -- and it is what
the second half of Unique-Root needs.  That invariant says the writer observes
every reachable node as an iterator, and both rules revoke exactly one iterator
observation, so without the unreachability result it fails at the very step that
revokes it.  Unique-Reachable does not supply this: it says the result is still
a tree, not which nodes are in it, and this is the one place where the
difference matters.  T-Insert, which revokes nothing, needs neither.

And the rest of the protocol is machine-checked as whole actions.  SyncStop is
where the empty bounding set is spent: Readers-In-Free-List says every thread in
an entry bounds a reclamation, and afterwards there are none, so every entry is
empty -- which is exactly the conjunct the *freeable* denotation asks for,
obtained rather than assumed.  ReadEnd is discussed above.  Of the four steps in
the reclamation chain three are now complete; SyncStart has its
Free-List-Reachability case and not yet the rest.

We will expand Section 6 along these lines.
